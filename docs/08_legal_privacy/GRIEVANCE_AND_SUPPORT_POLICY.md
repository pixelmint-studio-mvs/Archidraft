## 3. GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL WORKFLOW: STATUTORY OBLIGATION VS. OPERATIONAL TARGETS

### 3.1. Binding Statutory Resolution Timeline
Under **Rule 5(9) of the IT (SPDI) Rules, 2011**, the Grievance Officer **shall redress discrepancies and grievances of data providers within one month (30 days)** from the date of receipt of the grievance. This 30-day resolution deadline is a legally binding statutory obligation under Indian law.

### 3.2. Proposed Internal Workflow Stages *(Operational Guidelines)*
To achieve compliance with the statutory 30-day mandate, Archi Draft targets the following internal operational progression:

```
Stage 1: Receipt & Verification (Target: 0–48 Hours)
   │
   ▼
Stage 2: Logging & Ticket Assignment (Target: 48 Hours)
   │
   ▼
Stage 3: Technical / Operational Investigation (Target: Days 3–15)
   │
   ▼
Stage 4: Proposed Resolution & User Consultation (Target: Days 16–25)
   │
   ▼
Stage 5: Final Resolution & Closure (Mandatory Statutory Deadline: Within 30 Days Maximum)
```

- **Receipt & Verification:** The Grievance Officer verifies that the sender is the registered account holder associated with the project or data in question.
- **Logging:** A unique tracking number is assigned (e.g., `GRIEV-YYYY-XXXX`) and acknowledged to the complainant within 48 hours.
- **Investigation:** Relevant system audit logs (`activity_logs` in D1), drawing version histories, or collaboration messages are reviewed confidentially.
- **Closure:** A formal written resolution report is communicated to the complainant within the mandatory 30-day statutory window.

## 4. ESCALATION MATRIX

If a user is dissatisfied with an initial operational support response, the matter may be escalated as follows:

1. **Level 1 — Operational Support:** Handled by standard customer support (`[SUPPORT EMAIL — TO BE CONFIRMED]`).
2. **Level 2 — Grievance Officer:** Formal review by the designated Grievance Officer (`[GRIEVANCE EMAIL — TO BE CONFIRMED]`).
3. **Level 3 — Executive / Sole Proprietor:** Escalation to the Sole Proprietor and Legal Counsel.
4. **External Regulatory Recourse:** If a data protection grievance remains unresolved following internal redressal, the user may approach the **Data Protection Board of India (DPBI)** in accordance with the provisions and rules under the DPDP Act, 2023.
